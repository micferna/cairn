Cairn counts your steps, distance, elevation gain and travel modes — walking,
running, cycling, scooter, car, train, plane — without ever recording where you
have been.

The app does not declare the INTERNET permission. The operating system therefore
denies it any network socket: it cannot transmit anything, even if its code were
compromised. Verify with "aapt dump permissions".

When GPS is enabled (optional, off by default), coordinates live in RAM only
long enough to compute the delta between two points, then are overwritten. No
track ever reaches disk.

Instead of a map, Cairn translates your numbers into equivalences: "412 km, that
is Paris to Lyon", "4,812 m of climb, that is Mont Blanc". You have never been
to Lyon — that is precisely the point.

An in-app transparency ledger records every disk write and every sensor opened.

No ads, no account, no third-party analytics, no cloud backup.
